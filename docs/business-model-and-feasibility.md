# Radio Ad Capture: business model and feasibility

Date: 2026-11-12. First pass, based on a short review of trade press and
vendor announcements. Figures marked "vendor-reported" come from the
companies selling the product and have not been independently checked.

## The concept

A listener in a car hears a radio ad and, with one press or a spoken
command, has the ad's details sent to their phone. The listener gets
convenience; the advertiser gets a counted response; the station gets
proof that its ads work.

## Verdict

The concept is sound and the timing is good, but an idea on its own is
hard to sell. What can be sold or licensed is one of three things:
protected intellectual property, a working pilot with response data, or
signed station partners. The recommended path is a small pilot with one
station group, with a patent review done before pitching anyone.

## Why the timing is good

- **Radio still owns the car.** AM/FM is about 54 to 56% of all in-car
  audio time and about 83 to 85% of in-car ad-supported audio (Edison
  Share of Ear, 2025, as summarised by Westwood One and Radio Ink).
- **It is a large market that cannot prove response.** US local radio
  advertising is about $12.5 billion in 2026 (BIA). Attribution today is
  indirect: tracked phone numbers, promo codes, or matching ad air times
  against website traffic.
- **The dashboard already shows the advertiser.** Quu says over 80% of US
  vehicles can show station text and over half can show images. Xperi's
  DTS AutoStage is in about 16 million vehicles and about a quarter of the
  100 top-selling US models.
- **The response step is the missing piece.** No source found shows Quu or
  Xperi offering a way for the listener to act on a broadcast ad. SiriusXM
  says clickable in-car ad buttons are coming, and Audacy describes in-car
  radio ads as becoming "interactive and actionable", so others are
  heading the same way.
- **Interactive audio ads get used.** Phone-based formats report response
  rates of roughly 1 to 4% (AdsWizz ShakeMe) and higher for voice replies
  (Instreamatic), all vendor-reported and none measured in a car.

## Who would pay

| Customer | What they buy | How they pay | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radio station groups | "Actionable" ad spots they can sell at a premium, plus response reports | Monthly fee per station, plus a fee per response | They own the advertiser relationship and the data on what is airing. Most realistic first customer. |
| Advertisers | Leads: people who asked for the offer | Per response, billed through the station | Simple to explain. Price depends on the category (a car dealer lead is worth far more than a pizza coupon). |
| Dashboard platforms (Xperi, Quu) | The response feature for screens they already reach | Licence or acquisition | They have the route to the car screen and lack the button. Most likely buyer of the whole thing. |
| Carmakers | A feature and a share of ad revenue | Revenue share | Slow sales cycles (years). Not a first customer. |
| Listeners | Nothing | Free | Charging listeners would kill adoption. |

**Recommended model:** sell to station groups, priced per station plus per
response, and treat a dashboard platform as the eventual partner or buyer.

## Feasibility

| Area | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Core technology | Straightforward | The demo already does it. Stations' playout systems already publish what is airing to feed dashboards. |
| Knowing which ad is on air | Needs a station partner | Only the station knows reliably. Without one, the fallback is recognising ads by sound, which is harder and less reliable. |
| Getting a button on the car's own radio screen | Hard | Apps cannot add to the built-in radio screen. This needs Xperi, Quu or a carmaker. |
| Button inside a station's own app (CarPlay, Android Auto) | Feasible | When the listener streams through the app, the app controls the screen. Limited to app listeners, a minority of radio listening. |
| Voice command | Feasible | An app can offer "send this ad to my phone" through the phone's assistant. Works in any car. |
| Linking the car to a phone | Needs an app or account | The same way a TV knows which phone to send to: both are signed in. Text messages are the fallback and carry consent rules and per-message cost. |
| Legal | Needs advice | Existing patents, text-message consent rules, driver-distraction guidelines, privacy of listening data. |

## Main risks

1. **Existing patents.** At least two US patents touch this area: one held
   by Auddia on digital audio services, and one titled "Vehicle radio
   displaying information associated with broadcast on mobile device"
   (US 9,819,777, owner not confirmed). Earlier car-computer patents also
   describe a button that fetches an ad's contact details. A patent
   attorney should check what is free to build and what could be
   protected.
2. **A platform builds it first.** Xperi, Quu, SiriusXM or a large station
   group could add this themselves. Speed and a pilot are the defence.
3. **Chicken and egg.** Stations want listeners using it; listeners need
   stations offering it. Starting inside one station group's existing app
   avoids building an audience from nothing.
4. **Low in-car response.** Nobody has published response rates for
   broadcast radio in a car. A pilot is the only way to find out.

## Suggested next steps

1. **Patent attorney, before pitching.** A freedom-to-operate check and,
   if there is something new to claim, a provisional filing.
2. **Five conversations.** Sales managers at local stations and, if
   possible, someone at Quu or Xperi. One question: would advertisers pay
   more for a spot that reports how many listeners asked for the offer?
3. **One pilot.** A single station or small group, a handful of
   advertisers, four to eight weeks. Build only what the pilot needs: the
   station feed, the listener's saved-ads app, and the advertiser report.
4. **Decide with data.** Response rate and advertiser reaction determine
   whether to build a company, license it, or sell it.

## Not yet researched

- Response-rate evidence specific to cars.
- What stations currently charge for dashboard visual ads.
- Full patent landscape and claim details.
- Carmaker and Apple or Google rules for interactive ads on screens while
  driving.
- Lead prices advertisers pay in each category.
